Classification Report

  • Earth observation (EO) has long transcended its origins in cartography and weather forecasting. Today, multi-temporal satellite remote sensing — the systematic acquisition of imagery over the same area at repeated intervals — forms the backbone of conflict damage monitoring. When cities are subjected to armed conflict, rapid and objective damage assessment is critical for humanitarian response, reconstruction planning, and accountability documentation. Satellite-based longitudinal analysis offers a scalable, cost-effective, and above all safe alternative to ground surveys in active conflict zones.

  • Most operational assessments follow the JDAMS (Joint Damage Assessment and Munitions Scale) or UNOSAT's five-tier classification system. These are increasingly automated through machine learning pipelines that ingest multi-temporal image stacks and output georeferenced damage polygons compatible with standard GIS formats (GeoJSON, Shapefile). The integration of change vector analysis across multiple spectral bands — rather than a single pre/post comparison — improves temporal robustness by filtering out seasonal and illumination-driven false positives.
